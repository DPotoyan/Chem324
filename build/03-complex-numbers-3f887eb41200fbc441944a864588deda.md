# HW 3: Complex Numbers

### Question-1

Given $z = x + 4iy$ find:

- $\operatorname{Re}(z)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(z)$ (_real and imaginary parts of $z$_)
- $\operatorname{Re}(z^{*})$ and $\operatorname{Im}(z^{*})$ (_real and imaginary parts of the conjugate $z^{*}$_)
- $\operatorname{Re}(z^2)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(z^2)$
- $\operatorname{Re}(z \cdot z^{*})$ and $\operatorname{Im}(z \cdot z^{*})$

### Question-2

Convert the following complex numbers from polar form $z = r e^{i\phi}$ to Cartesian form $z = x + iy$:

- $z = e^{i\pi/4}$
- $z = e^{-2\pi i} + e^{4\pi i}$

### Question-3

Show that the functions $\Phi_m(\phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{im\phi}$ with an integer parameter (e.g. $m = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$) satisfy the following relations:

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \Phi_m^{*}(\phi)\, \Phi_n(\phi)\, d\phi = 0 \qquad \text{when } n \neq m,
$$

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \Phi_m^{*}(\phi)\, \Phi_m(\phi)\, d\phi = 1 \qquad \text{when } n = m.
$$

### Question-4

Using Euler's relation for complex numbers, show that the following equality holds when $n \neq m$:

$$
\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi} \cos(nx) \cdot \sin(mx)\, dx = 0
$$

### Question-5

Show that the sine and cosine functions can be expressed in terms of complex exponentials as follows:

- $\cos\phi = \dfrac{e^{i\phi} + e^{-i\phi}}{2}$
- $\sin\phi = \dfrac{e^{i\phi} - e^{-i\phi}}{2i}$
